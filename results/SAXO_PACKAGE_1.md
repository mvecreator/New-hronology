# SAXO-PACKAGE-1 — Gate result

Status: **CLOSED / significant result**  
Date: 2026-09-20

## Result

**Structural distinctiveness of SAXO_RETURN_1: YES.**  
**Uniqueness of the shared Gospel/Saxo package: FAIL.**

The Saxo episode remains a high-quality structural correspondence because it preserves the same protagonist, advance planning, temporal structure, death-status, memorial action, return, order, and high narrative centrality.

However, the international control search found multiple independent narrative/ritual mechanisms reproducing large parts of the same causal-predictive package. Therefore the package can no longer be treated as a near-unique signature of a Gospel source.

This does **not** refute HC. It materially raises the background probability under HB and reduces the evidential weight that can be assigned to SAXO_RETURN_1 by rarity alone.

## Frozen atoms

- **K1** — advance protocol / prediction concerning the death-return phase;
- **K2** — temporal interval or trigger, with explicit distinction between forecast interval and merely elapsed interval;
- **K3** — actual, apparent, mistaken, or symbolic death-state;
- **K4** — formal mourning, funeral, burial, or memorial action;
- **K5** — the same protagonist returns alive / is revived;
- **K6** — an earlier protocol or prediction is fulfilled and causally linked to the result;
- **K7** — the package is narratively central.

Additional tags:
- `death_type`
- `agency`
- `time_status`

A rare Saxo-only feature that is absent from the Gospel must **not** be counted as evidence for Gospel origin. In particular, Amleth's direct appearance at his own memorial remains important for the genealogy of the Amleth story, but is not a Gospel-shared anchor.

## Baseline

### Saxo / Amleth

Saxo explicitly states that before departure Amleth instructed his mother to arrange pretended obsequies for him one year later and promised to return. After a whole year he returns to Jutland and enters the banquet-room while his own obsequies are being held; the false report of his death had spread. This return immediately enables the revenge climax.

Primary working text:
https://internetshakespeare.uvic.ca/doc/Saxo_M/complete/index.html

### Gospel comparison

The Synoptic tradition explicitly places predictions of suffering/death and rising after/on the third day before the Passion; Mark 14:28 also predicts a post-resurrection meeting in Galilee.

Working references:
https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Mark%208%3A31
https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+14%3A28

## Strong controls

### 1. Inanna's Descent — strongest control found so far

Before descending, Inanna gives Ninshubur explicit instructions: lament for her, approach a sequence of gods, and ultimately seek Enki, whom she expects to restore her to life. Inanna is later explicitly turned into a corpse and hung on a hook. After three days and three nights have passed, Ninshubur executes the instructions and performs ritual lamentation. Enki's rescue procedure revives Inanna; after her ascent she explicitly says that Ninshubur did not neglect her instructions and brought her back to life.

**Critical correction:** the text does **not** say that Inanna predicted a three-day resurrection interval. Three days and nights elapse before Ninshubur begins the instructed rescue sequence; the time of actual reanimation is later and unspecified. This distinction is mandatory.

Working primary translation:
https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/section1/tr141.htm

Physical/composite witness catalogue:
https://cdli.earth/P468903

CDLI assigns the witnesses to the Old Babylonian period, ca. 1900–1600 BCE, in standard Assyriological chronology. For this project, chronological priority over the Gospel is **not assumed** from that label alone; dating must be evaluated in a separate chronology/archaeometry gate.

Methodological discussion cautioning against treating the three days as the exact death-to-resurrection interval:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/considerations-of-methodology-in-the-study-of-the-mystery-religions-and-early-christianity/70B1E9D3CC1554026D31258301513E5A

### 2. Nisga'a / Niska Olala initiation

Frazer, citing Boas, describes a ritual in which an initiate is symbolically killed, a decapitated dummy substitutes for him, women mourn, relatives hold a funeral banquet and burn the effigy, the novice remains absent for a whole year, and then returns alive.

This is especially important for the **ritual/funerary + fixed one-year interval + return** half of the Saxo package. It is an institutional ritual script rather than a protagonist's personal prophecy.

Working source:
https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/43433/pg43433-images.html

### 3. Romeo and Juliet

Friar Laurence specifies in advance that Juliet will appear dead for **42 hours**, will be carried on a bier to the Capulet vault, and will then awaken. The operational rescue plan fails because communication fails, but the planned causal structure closely reproduces:
`advance protocol → exact interval → apparent death → burial → same protagonist awakens`.

Working text:
https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/romeo-and-juliet/read/4/1/

### 4. Savitri and Satyavan

Satyavan's death is predicted exactly one year in advance. Savitri lives with that prediction, prepares as the date approaches, witnesses his actual death, follows Yama and wins Satyavan's life back. There is no formal funeral/burial, so this is not a full package match.

Working text:
https://sacred-texts.com/hin/m03/m03295.htm

### 5. Alcestis

Alcestis actually dies and is buried; Heracles fights Death near the tomb and returns the same Alcestis alive. A third-day purification appears **after** her return and must not be mis-scored as a predicted resurrection interval.

Working text:
https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Aabo%3Atlg%2C0006%2C002%3A1128

### 6. Much Ado About Nothing — Hero

After Hero collapses, the Friar explicitly orders that she be hidden, declared dead, publicly mourned, commemorated with epitaphs, and given all burial rites. The false-death plan is later resolved by Hero's live reappearance.

Working text:
https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/much-ado-about-nothing/read/4/1/

### 7. Tom Sawyer

The boys are believed drowned; a formal funeral service is held; Tom deliberately arranges to attend his own funeral and the supposedly dead boys enter the church during the service. It strongly matches the middle/end of Saxo but lacks a pre-departure fixed interval or death-return prophecy.

Working text:
https://www.gutenberg.org/files/7196/7196-h/7196-h.htm

### 8. Apparent-death tale tradition

ATU 990 independently establishes `apparent death → burial → awakening → return home` as a catalogued folktale type.

Working catalogue:
https://www.mythologydatabase.com/db_atu.php?atu=990

Ancient Greek romance also contains apparent-death/burial/revival machinery; Xenophon of Ephesus' Anthia drinks what she thinks is poison, is mourned and buried, then wakes alive in the tomb:
https://www.elfinspell.com/ClassicalTexts/Rooke/XenophonsEphesianHistory/Ephesiaca-Book3.html

## Interpretation

The controls split into two recurring generators:

### A. Ritual/funerary generator
`symbolic/apparent death → mourning/funeral → absence → return`

Examples: Nisga'a initiation, Tom Sawyer, Hero, Juliet, ATU 990.

### B. Prophecy/death-return generator
`prediction/foreknowledge → death → intervention → same protagonist returns`

Examples: Inanna, Savitri/Satyavan, Alcestis.

**Saxo is distinctive because it fuses both generators**:
`self-authored advance plan + exact one-year interval + social death + own memorial + live return + climax`.

But because both component generators and near-complete combinations occur independently, rarity alone can no longer carry a large likelihood ratio for HC.

## Effect on hypotheses

- **H0:** remains inadequate as a complete explanation; the packages are structured, not just lexical coincidences.
- **HB:** strengthened materially. International ritual, folklore and literary mechanisms generate much of the observed structure.
- **HC:** remains possible, but SAXO_RETURN_1 is no longer admissible as a near-unique fingerprint. HC now needs additional discriminators that HB predicts poorly.

## New methodological consequence

Future likelihood calculations must separate:

1. **Gospel-shared core** — only features present in both source and proposed prototype may support HC;
2. **Saxo-specific rarity** — useful for genealogy of Amleth, but not evidence for Gospel origin unless a transformation rule was independently frozen elsewhere;
3. **chronological priority** — must be tested independently rather than inherited from conventional labels.

`SAXO-PACKAGE-1 = DISTINCTIVE_MATCH_PASS / UNIQUENESS_FAIL`
